Manhattan Prime Neighborhood

Where Manhattan's luxury market lives.

Six neighborhoods that define the top of the Manhattan market — each with its own architecture, buyer profile, and long-term value drivers.

Tribeca

A historic and trendy Manhattan neighborhood, Tribeca pairs cast-iron loft buildings with some of the city's most sought-after new-development condominiums. Cobblestone streets, large pre-war floorplans, and proximity to the Hudson River waterfront make it a magnet for families and buyers seeking scale and privacy without leaving Downtown.

Billionaires' Row

Manhattan's ultimate luxury address, Billionaires' Row runs along 57th Street and its surrounding blocks, home to some of the tallest and most exclusive residential towers in the world. Full-floor residences, record-setting sale prices, and unobstructed Central Park views define this corridor's appeal to the most discerning global buyers.

Hudson Yards

Manhattan's modern marvel, Hudson Yards is the city's newest large-scale district — a master-planned campus of glass towers, private residences, retail, and public art built above the West Side rail yards. It offers new-construction amenities, floor-to-ceiling views, and a level of building infrastructure unmatched elsewhere in the city.

Midtown Manhattan

The heart of New York City, Midtown combines commercial density with an increasingly residential luxury market, from Central Park South to the corridors surrounding Fifth and Park Avenue. Its central location, hotel-branded residences, and cultural landmarks make it a natural base for buyers who divide their time between business and leisure.

SoHo

Manhattan's trendiest neighborhood, SoHo is defined by cast-iron architecture, converted loft buildings, and a concentration of design, fashion, and gallery culture found nowhere else in the city. Its boutique condominiums and landmarked buildings appeal to buyers seeking character alongside scarcity.

Upper West Side

Manhattan's elegant and family-friendly neighborhood, the Upper West Side is anchored by Central Park and Riverside Park, pre-war cooperative buildings, and some of the city's top private and public schools. It remains a preferred choice for families and buyers seeking a quieter, more residential pace within easy reach of Midtown.
