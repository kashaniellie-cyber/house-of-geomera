Foreign Buyer Activity · 2024–2025

Foreign buyers in NYC's luxury real estate market.

An evidence-based analysis of international buyer activity relevant to properties priced at approximately $4M and above, drawn from public market reporting.

This report examines general luxury-market trends, international buyer activity, nationalities, property preferences, motivations, and key statistics relevant to New York City's luxury segment. Public market reports do not provide a complete nationality-by-nationality breakdown for foreign purchases of NYC properties priced at $4M+; accordingly, Manhattan luxury statistics and national foreign-buyer statistics are presented separately below. Figures from the National Association of REALTORS® (NAR) apply to the United States as a whole and should not be interpreted as NYC-only data.

General Trends in the Luxury Market

No credible public source reports the percentage of NYC luxury transactions involving foreign buyers specifically in the $4M+ segment. At the national level, foreign buyers accounted for approximately 1.3% of U.S. existing-home purchases by number and 2.0% by dollar volume during April 2023–March 2024. During April 2024–March 2025, these shares increased to 1.9% by number and 2.5% by dollar volume — figures covering all U.S. existing-home transactions, not only luxury properties or New York City.

Foreign buyers purchased approximately 54,300 U.S. existing homes valued at $42B during the 2024 reporting period (April 2023–March 2024) — the lowest figure NAR has recorded since it began producing estimates in 2009. That rebounded sharply in the 2025 reporting period (April 2024–March 2025), with foreign buyers purchasing approximately 78,100 homes valued at $56B: a 44% increase in purchase count and 33.2% increase in dollar volume, the first annual increase since 2017. The average purchase price paid by foreign buyers nationwide was approximately $719,000 in the 2025 period, a decline of 7.9% from the year before — though there is no verified public figure specific to NYC's $4M+ segment.

Ultra-Luxury Properties ($10M+)

The ultra-luxury market showed strong but uneven activity through 2025. January recorded 67 contracts above $5M (+5% YoY), with contracts above $10M up 85% YoY. April recorded 79 contracts above $5M (+3% YoY) as active listings fell 10%. June recorded 83 contracts above $5M (+14% YoY), with active inventory down 18% — the lowest June level in ten years. Activity softened later in the year: September recorded 57 contracts above $5M (−12% YoY, though still 16% above its 10-year average), and December recorded 65 contracts (−17% YoY, only 2% below its 10-year average).

Property Type Preferences

Condominiums are generally more accessible to international purchasers than cooperatives, since condo transactions ordinarily do not require board approval — though buyers remain subject to the building's financial requirements, title review, and applicable reporting rules.

Cooperative apartments may impose financial, residency, financing, and interview requirements that vary by building, often making them less practical for buyers based primarily outside the U.S.

New developments offer sponsor sales, modern amenities, and fewer resale-board issues. New-development transactions represented 14.3% of Manhattan sales in Q4 2024 (median price ~$2.428M) and 15.3% in Q4 2025, up 12.6% YoY as inventory fell 13.3%.

Trophy properties — penthouses, full-floor residences, and townhouses — can command significant premiums for scarcity and view, though public data does not establish what share of trophy buyers were foreign during 2024–2025.

Motivations for Foreign Buyers

Wealth preservation: Knight Frank reported cash purchases at 68% of Manhattan transactions in Q4 2023, describing New York as a location for diversification. Cash share reached 62.3% in Q4 2024 and 64.7% in Q4 2025 (vs. a 10-year average of 52.5%) — figures covering the overall market, not citizenship-specific data.

Cash purchasing: Approximately 90% of Manhattan sales above $3M were cash purchases in Q1 2025, reflecting the reduced role of mortgage financing at the top of the market.

Personal and family use: NAR data shows resident foreign buyers accounted for approximately 56% of foreign-buyer home purchases nationally in the 2025 period.

Education, lifestyle, and business connectivity are commonly cited qualitative drivers, though no reliable public source quantifies their weight for NYC luxury buyers specifically.

Neighborhood Concentration

Public luxury reports track Manhattan as a market but do not consistently disclose purchaser nationality by neighborhood. Neighborhoods containing significant inventory above $4M include Central Park South and Midtown (high-rise condominiums, Central Park views), Tribeca (large condominiums and loft conversions), the Upper East Side (townhouses and pre-war cooperatives), SoHo and Greenwich Village (loft and boutique condominiums), and Chelsea and Hudson Yards (new-development high-rises). These reflect available inventory, not a documented nationality preference.

Geopolitical & Economic Influences

Exchange-rate movements affect buyers differently depending on their home currency; it is not accurate to generalize that the dollar's movement affected all foreign purchasers uniformly. Foreign cash buyers are less directly exposed to U.S. mortgage rates than financed purchasers, though rates still shape the broader competitive landscape — the overall Manhattan cash share reached 64.7% in Q4 2025, with the condo cash share at 74.4%. Foreign individuals are generally permitted to purchase New York real estate, subject to sanctions, anti-money-laundering, tax, and transaction-reporting requirements, alongside any documentation standards imposed by cooperative boards or lenders.
